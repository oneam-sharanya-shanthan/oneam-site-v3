# OneAM Early Pay Banner Ad Design Guide

## Purpose

This guide translates banner-ad principles into a visual system for OneAM Early Pay. The goal is to help business owners understand the offer within a few seconds:

> OneAM helps businesses access cash from their invoices in days instead of waiting 30, 60, or 90 days for customers to pay.

The banners should feel modern, credible, direct, and business-friendly. They should not look like generic lending, credit card, or high-pressure financing ads.

## Core Design Strategy

### Communicate one transformation

The clearest visual story for OneAM is:

**Waiting 60–90 days → accessing cash in days**

Build each banner around one primary idea. Do not try to communicate speed, flexibility, control, pricing, customer privacy, and the absence of debt in the same ad.

The five approved short-copy concepts can be organized into three benefit territories:

1. **Speed:** Access cash in days, not months.
2. **Control:** Get paid early without giving up control.
3. **Business continuity:** Slow payments do not have to slow down the business.

### Make Early Pay identifiable

“Early Pay” should appear either in the main message or as a clear descriptor beside the OneAM logo. Short phrases such as “Skip the wait” are stronger when viewers can immediately identify what OneAM offers.

Suggested brand lockup:

```text
OneAM
EARLY PAY
```

## Visual Hierarchy

Every banner should contain three primary components:

1. **Brand:** OneAM logo with Early Pay identified
2. **Offer:** One headline or headline/subtext pair
3. **Action:** One prominent CTA button

The preferred reading order is:

```text
Logo → Primary benefit → Supporting detail → CTA
```

Email and contact-form icons are secondary utilities. Place them at the bottom or far edge of the layout and give them less visual weight than the main CTA.

### Type hierarchy

- Give the headline the largest type and strongest contrast.
- Keep subtext noticeably smaller, but readable at the banner's actual display size.
- Avoid thin weights and italics.
- Keep text to four lines or fewer; two or three is preferable.
- Design and review the type at the banner's actual 990 × 93 display size.
- Emphasize only one phrase, such as **in days**, **60–90 days**, or **Early Pay**.

## Recommended Visual Concepts

### 1. Time comparison

Use a direct comparison to make the value proposition understandable at a glance.

```text
60–90 DAYS         DAYS
waiting        →   with OneAM Early Pay
```

Possible copy:

> OneAM Early Pay: Access cash in days, not months.

This should be the lead concept because it converts an abstract financial service into a visible difference in time. Make the numbers larger than the supporting language.

### 2. Invoice-to-cash progression

Use a simple visual sequence rather than a generic business photograph:

```text
Invoice sent → OneAM Early Pay → Cash accessed
```

Possible copy:

> Early pay for your invoices—fast, fair, and flexible.

Use a clean invoice card, directional line, and cash-available state. The treatment should resemble a modern financial platform, not a literal pile of money.

### 3. Control and choice

Show several invoice cards with one selected. This makes the website's “choose which invoices to fund, and when” benefit tangible.

Possible copy:

> Get paid early on your invoices—without giving up control.

The selected invoice can receive a restrained highlight in the brand accent color. Avoid showing every invoice as automatically funded.

### 4. Skip the wait

Use a long muted payment timeline interrupted by a short, bright OneAM path.

Possible copy:

> Skip the wait. Get paid early.

Supporting line:

> Access cash in days while customers pay as usual.

The visual should explain that the customer's normal payment schedule remains unchanged.

### 5. Keep business moving

Use relevant operational imagery to connect cash flow with business continuity.

Possible copy:

> Slow payments don't have to slow you down.

Supporting line:

> Unlock cash flow from revenue you've already earned.

Choose imagery of real business activity—inventory moving, equipment operating, orders being fulfilled, or teams working. Avoid handshakes, credit cards, piggy banks, coins, and generic stock-market charts.

## Addressing Skepticism

Early Pay may be unfamiliar, and viewers may assume it is a loan or traditional factoring. A compact reassurance layer can address one concern per creative.

Possible trust lines:

```text
No debt • No equity given up • No customer disruption
```

or

```text
Choose your invoices • No commitments • Stay in control
```

Do not use both lines in one banner. If the 93-pixel height becomes crowded, omit the trust line instead of making it unreadably small.

## Imagery and Graphic Style

### Use

- Clear invoice, calendar, timeline, or selection graphics
- Real operational environments relevant to the audience
- Simple interface-inspired cards that make the service visible
- Directional movement from invoice to available cash
- White space and a restrained, professional composition
- One strong brand accent used to guide attention

### Avoid

- Generic lending and banking imagery
- Dollar-sign overload or literal piles of cash
- Busy collages and decorative elements without meaning
- Multiple competing illustrations
- Imagery that implies OneAM takes over customer relationships
- Urgency devices such as countdown clocks unless there is a real deadline

## Color and Contrast

- Use OneAM's navy as the credibility anchor.
- Use fuchsia or teal selectively for the key number, directional cue, or CTA.
- Maintain strong contrast between text and background.
- Give the CTA a distinct filled treatment that still belongs to the brand system.
- Do not use every brand color at equal strength in one banner.
- Maintain enough edge definition and contrast for the banner to remain distinct on both light and dark publisher backgrounds.

## CTA System

All primary CTA buttons link to [https://www.oneam.us](https://www.oneam.us).

Approved CTA labels:

- **Get paid early**
- **Explore Early Pay**
- **See how it works**
- **Get started**
- **Book a 5-min demo**

For broad awareness campaigns, prefer **Explore Early Pay** or **See how it works**. They suit a considered B2B decision and accurately describe what happens after clicking to the main site.

Use **Get paid early** when the benefit is already clearly explained in the banner. Reserve **Book a 5-min demo** for qualified or retargeting audiences.

Secondary icon destinations:

- **Email:** [info@oneam.us](mailto:info@oneam.us)
- **Contact form:** [https://www.oneam.us/contact](https://www.oneam.us/contact)

The icons should not compete with the primary CTA. If the 990 × 93 layout becomes crowded, remove the secondary icons before reducing the primary message or CTA.

## 990 × 93 Layout

The campaign uses one horizontal size: **990 × 93 pixels**. Design directly at this size rather than adapting a taller composition.

### Recommended structure

Use four clear horizontal zones:

```text
[Logo / Early Pay]  [Primary message]  [CTA]  [Email / Contact icons]
```

- Reserve roughly 15–20% of the width for the OneAM/Early Pay lockup.
- Give roughly 50–55% to the primary message.
- Reserve roughly 15–20% for the CTA.
- Use the remaining space for the small email and contact-form icons, if they remain legible.
- Use one-line copy whenever possible.
- A headline/subtext version should use no more than two compact lines in total.
- Keep the message vertically centered and preserve comfortable top and bottom padding.
- Use a divider, spacing, or subtle color-block change to separate the brand, message, and action zones.
- Avoid detailed photography; at only 93 pixels high, it will become visual noise.
- Prefer simple timelines, invoice icons, arrows, or large numerical contrasts.
- Keep **60–90 days**, **in days**, or **Early Pay** as the single emphasized element.
- If space is tight, remove trust markers and contact icons before reducing headline legibility.
- Confirm the full banner remains understandable when viewed at 100% size, not enlarged.

## Motion Guidelines

Animation can help show the process, but it should explain rather than decorate.

Recommended sequence:

1. An invoice appears.
2. “60–90 days” is shown as the normal wait.
3. OneAM Early Pay shortens the path to “days.”
4. The final frame holds the full value proposition, logo, and CTA.

Keep motion restrained and the story understandable without animation. The final frame should remain visible long enough to read and should work as a static banner on its own.

Avoid constant pulsing, flashing buttons, rapid transitions, and multiple elements moving simultaneously.

## Recommended Campaign Direction

Start with three coordinated concepts:

1. **Speed:** “OneAM Early Pay: Access cash in days, not months.”
2. **Control:** “Get paid early on your invoices—without giving up control.”
3. **Continuity:** “Slow payments don't have to slow you down.”

Use the same brand system and CTA treatment across all three, but give each a distinct visual idea. This keeps the campaign recognizable while communicating speed, control, and business continuity.

## Final Design Checklist

- Can the offer be understood in two or three seconds?
- Is Early Pay explicitly identified?
- Is there only one primary message?
- Is the most important benefit the largest element?
- Does the imagery explain or reinforce the service?
- Is the CTA prominent and easy to find?
- Are the email and contact icons visually secondary?
- Is all text readable at actual display size?
- Does the full message remain clear within the 990 × 93 composition?
- Does the banner feel like modern B2B finance rather than generic lending?
- Does the click destination match the CTA's promise?
